## What counts as a data plate

A data plate is a label or stamped plate applied by the manufacturer that
carries **identifying data**: a model number, a serial number, electrical or
capacity ratings, certification marks, a manufacturer's address. It usually
looks like a form — labelled fields with values beside them.

These all count, and none of them has to be tidy:

- A printed label, an etched metal plate, or text moulded into the casing.
- A blank form filled in by hand in pen at installation.
- A small sticker carrying only a serial number.
- A plate that is scratched, faded, partly peeled, glared out, at an angle, or
  partly hidden behind a pipe. **Hard to read is still a data plate.** Whether
  you can read it is the next task's problem, not this one's.
- More than one plate in the frame. Answer `yes` if at least one is a data plate.

## What does not count

- **Product branding on a housing.** A brand name and a model name moulded or
  stuck onto the equipment's cover is how the manufacturer sells it, not how it
  identifies that individual unit. This is the most common thing mistaken for a
  data plate: it carries a make and a model and no serial, no ratings, and no
  form structure. If what you see is a logo and a product name on a painted
  cover, that is `no`, even though you could read a make and model off it.
- Warning, hazard or safety placards — DANGER, electric shock, hot water.
- Registration, warranty, service-company or inspection stickers.
- Energy-rating and certification-body labels standing on their own.
- Wiring diagrams and instruction plates with no identifying data.
- A photograph of equipment where no label is legible at all.
