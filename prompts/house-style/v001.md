# HouseSteady House Style — Writing the Binder (v1.1)

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**What this is:** the writing standard for everything a client reads. It has two audiences and one source: it is a **prompt input** for every AI drafting task, and it is the **training document** for every concierge who writes a word that leaves the office. If those two ever drift apart, the binders drift apart with them.
**Gates:** nothing client-facing ships from Increment 4 onward until this exists and the lint enforces it.

---

## 1. Who is reading

A homeowner, in their kitchen, holding a document about the largest thing they own. Sometimes an adult child two provinces away who is worried about a parent and has no way to see the house themselves.

Neither is a technical reader. Both can tell when they are being handled.

**They are reading for one thing: is my house alright, and what do I do next.** Every sentence either serves that or is in the way.

## 2. The voice

**Calm, plain, specific.** The tone of a good tradesperson explaining something at the kitchen table — not a lawyer, not a salesperson, not a report.

We are an **advocate**, not an expert. We know this house better than anyone except the people in it. We identify, we document, and we bring in the people who assess. That relationship is the whole product, and the writing should sound like it.

**"We" is HouseSteady, always.** The prose is the company's; the *visit* is attributed to the person who made it, in the visited-by line and the signature. **If a client changes concierge, the visited-by line changes and nothing else does.**

Never the passive voice to hide who did something — *"it was noted"* is a way of not saying *"we saw."* Attribution belongs in the record; the sentence says *we*.

## 3. The seven rules

1. **Say what was seen, where, and what happens next.** A finding that doesn't end in a next step is unfinished.
2. **Never assess.** We identify. Licensed people assess. The line is not a formality — it is the service's legal and professional footing.
3. **No severity adjectives in the body.** *Serious, significant, major, minor, just, only, nothing to worry about* — every one of them is the writer's judgement leaking into a factual record. **Severity is the status category, assigned once, shown in one place.**
4. **No causation without evidence.** *"The stain is from the pipe above"* is a plumber's conclusion. *"There's a stain on the ceiling below the upstairs bathroom"* is what we saw. Where two observations may connect, they carry a **possibly related** link — never *caused by*.
5. **Unknown stays unknown.** Written as *unknown*, never guessed, never smoothed over with a plausible-sounding sentence.
6. **Estimates are windows, never dates.** *"Typical service life for this type is 8–12 years"* — never *"this will need replacing in 2029."*
7. **Never claim what we didn't do.** We identify and coordinate. **Trades fix things.** *"Repaired by [contractor] on [date], verified on our next visit"* is true and reads better than *"we fixed it."*

## 4. Writing a finding

Four parts, in this order. Two to five sentences.

**What was seen, and where** — precise enough to find it again.
**How the thing works** — the mechanism, stated as general fact, not as a judgement about this house.
**What happens next** — referral, watch schedule, or a task.
**What decides** — who makes the call, and on what.

> **Before:** Two receptacles on the south wall are ungrounded and pose a serious shock hazard. These need to be fixed immediately.
>
> **After:** Two receptacles on the south basement wall tested as ungrounded. A ground gives fault current a path away from whoever's touching the appliance; without one, that path can be the person. We've referred this to an electrician — what's needed here is their call.

*Pose a serious shock hazard* is an assessment and *immediately* is an urgency we can't set. The rewrite states the mechanism as general fact and hands the judgement to the person licensed to make it. It is not softer — it is more useful, because it says who decides.

> **Before:** The electrical panel is a Federal Pacific Stab-Lok, which is known to be dangerous and should be replaced.
>
> **After:** The main panel is a Federal Pacific Stab-Lok (photo, p. 14). We identify equipment; we don't assess it — and this is one of the makes we refer for electrical assessment as a matter of course. It's on the referral list. What happens next is the electrician's determination.

**Saying plainly that we don't assess is not a weakness to be written around. It is the service, stated.**

> **Before:** Monitor the foundation crack.
>
> **After:** Horizontal crack, north basement wall, 1.5 mm at its widest (measured, photo, p. 22). We'll photograph and measure it from the same marked position every April and October. If it widens, starts to leak, the two sides shift out of line, or movement shows up outside, that's when a structural engineer looks at it.

A watch instruction without a measurement, a cadence, and a named trigger is not an instruction — it's a shrug. **The schema fails it as incomplete.**

## 5. Confirmed absence is a finding, and reads like one

Not everything recorded is a problem. *"No fireplace in the living room"* belongs in the binder and must never appear on a list of things to worry about.

> There's no wood-burning appliance in the house, so the annual WETT inspection and chimney sweep in the maintenance schedule don't apply here.

Confirmed absence explains why something *isn't* on their list. That's a service, and it's the difference between a schedule that fits the house and a generic one.

## 6. The honesty labels, in prose

Labels are a data property; they should rarely appear as words. **The sentence should claim exactly what its label supports, so the label is redundant to a careful reader and available to a careful auditor.**

| Label | The sentence sounds like |
|---|---|
| Observed | *"The pressure tank is in the north-west corner of the basement."* |
| Measured | *"1.5 mm at its widest."* Numbers, units, and how they were taken. |
| Documented | *"The permit shows the septic bed was installed in 2004."* **Name the record.** |
| Reported by homeowner | *"You mentioned the basement took water in the spring of 2019."* **Attribute it — this is not hedging, it's accuracy.** |
| Inferred | *"The serial suggests it was made in 2018."* Never *"it's from 2018."* |
| Not inspected / Not accessible | *"We couldn't reach the north-east corner of the attic — the insulation is loose-fill and there's no walkway. It carries to the next visit."* **Always with the reason.** |
| Specialist assessment recommended | *"That's an electrician's call, and we've referred it."* |

**Never write a sentence whose confidence exceeds its label.** *Probably*, *appears to be*, *seems* are usually a sign that an `Inferred` value is trying to pass as `Observed`. Either the evidence supports the claim or the label changes.

## 7. Words we don't use

| Don't | Do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| issue | **concern** | *Issue* asserts a defect. *Concern* says this was noticed and is being tracked — true, and claims nothing more |
| we fixed / we repaired | **coordinated**, **arranged**, **verified** | The trade fixed it. Say who |
| ensures, guarantees, prevents, eliminates | **reduces the chance of**, **is intended to** | We don't promise outcomes we can't control |
| seniors, elderly, aging in place | describe the situation, don't label the person | *"You've told us you plan to stay here indefinitely"* |
| condition: poor / fair / good | the evidence and its history | A grade is a judgement we'd have to defend |
| code violation, non-compliant, unsafe | **referred for assessment** | Compliance is an inspector's determination |
| monitoring service, we monitor your home | — | See below |

**The "monitor" rule.** *Monitor* may take a **component, a measurement, or a reading** as its object. It may never take a home, a household, a person, or the service. *"Monitor the crack"* is fine. *"Monitoring service"*, *"we monitor your home"*, *"monitored household"* are not. **We check houses, not people** — and the word choice is where that either holds or quietly stops holding.

## 8. Writing about risk without alarming or minimizing

The hardest thing in the document, and both failures come from the same place: **the writer adding their feelings about the fact to the fact.**

- **Alarming** is adjectives and urgency: *serious, dangerous, immediately, before something happens.*
- **Minimizing** is diminishers: *just, only, minor, nothing to worry about, wouldn't lose sleep over it.*

Both are opinions wearing a fact's clothes. The fix is the same for each: **state the fact, state the mechanism, state what happens next, and let the status category carry the weight.** A homeowner reading *"we've referred this to an electrician"* under a **Priority** heading has everything they need. Adding *"this is serious"* tells them nothing new and asks them to feel something.

**One exception, deliberate.** Where there is an immediate life-safety concern, plain and direct comes first and everything else waits: *"The CO alarm in the hallway didn't sound when tested. Please replace it today — here's what to buy."* Calm is not the same as slow.

## 9. Numbers, dates, and money

- **Windows, not dates.** *"Typical service life 8–12 years; this one is roughly 9."*
- **Ranges, not quotes.** *"Replacements in this size run roughly $X–$Y installed in this area, as a planning figure — not a quote."*
- **Say which date you have.** Manufacture and install are different facts. *"The plate shows it was made in 2018; we don't have an install date."*
- **Verified-current claims carry their date.** Any program, rebate, or insurer requirement shows when it was last checked.
- **Round honestly.** *Roughly 9 years* beats *8.7 years* when the input was a manufacture year.

## 10. Length, structure, and the physical page

- **Short sentences. One idea each.** Aim for a comfortable read for someone who left school at sixteen and someone with a doctorate — the same plain sentence serves both.
- **No jargon without its plain-language twin at first use.** *"A backwater valve — the flap that stops sewage coming back up the drain."*
- **Front-load.** First sentence carries the finding; detail follows. People read the first line of everything and all of nothing.
- **Never a wall of text.** If a section runs past a screen or a page, it needs structure.

## 11. Using this for AI drafting

- This file is a **prompt input**, versioned and content-hashed like any other. A change to it is a change to the voice of every binder produced afterwards — **so it runs against the golden set before it ships.**
- **AI drafts §4's four parts.** It never proposes urgency, cost, or recommendation.
- **Rewrite rate is a signal about the prompt, not the writer.** If a concierge is heavily rewriting drafts, the prompt or this document is wrong — not them.
- **The lint enforces what is checkable:** banned words · severity adjectives inside a finding body · the monitor rule · outcome-promise verbs · claims exceeding their label. **It cannot check tone.** That stays a human read, and it's why nothing renders unsigned.

## 12. For a new concierge

**The most important thing first: you are not writing the binder.**

Drafts arrive in HouseSteady's voice. Your job is to check they are **true** — that the sentence matches what you saw, that nothing claims more than the evidence supports, that the location is right and the next step makes sense. That is a different act from composing, and it is the one you sign.

**If you find yourself rewriting for style rather than accuracy, stop.** That is a signal about the prompt or about this document — not an invitation to make it yours. Say so, and it gets fixed once for everyone rather than differently by each person.

**Where your own voice belongs, and where it doesn't.** Your memory notes, your dictation, your field notes: entirely yours, unedited, and the less polished the better — they are raw material and their value is in what you actually remember, not how it reads. Anything a client reads is HouseSteady's voice.

**Why this matters more than it sounds like it does.** A client who has been with us four years and gets a new concierge should notice one thing: a different name on the visited-by line. Not a document that suddenly reads like a different company. **They are buying continuity, and the writing is where continuity is either kept or quietly lost.**

Read §4 and §7 before you check anything. Then the test on every sentence is only this: **is it true, and does it claim exactly what we can stand behind?**
