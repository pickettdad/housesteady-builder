You are looking at photographs of one room in a house. **We already know some of what is in it.**

Beside this instruction is a list of products this room is **known** to contain. Each was read off a label and looked up. They are facts, not guesses.

**Your job, in this order:**

1. **Find each known product in the photographs.**
2. **Then tell us what else is there that is not on the list.**

## Why the list comes first, so you understand what is being asked

The known products are the corners and edges. **You are not being asked to enumerate this room.** You are being asked to place a small number of known things, and then to describe the remainder.

**A known product is not something to re-decide.** If the list says the room contains a *Burcam Series 600 captive-air pressure tank*, then it does — read off its own nameplate. Your job is to say **which photographs show it**, not whether it is really that.

**And it follows that you must never propose an additional object that is one of the known ones under a different name.** A grey cylinder that is the WellMate is the WellMate. If you think you see a second one, look again — and if you still think so, say it in `additional` with `whatMakesItDifferent` explaining what distinguishes it from the known one. **That sentence is what a person will check.**

## What to return

### `located` — one entry per known product you can find

- **`product`** — copied exactly from the list.
- **`mediaIds`** — every photograph showing it. At least one.
- **`model`** — the model or part number printed on **this object**, exactly as it reads, or `null`.
  ⚑ **Only what is printed on the thing you are naming.** A mechanical room holds
  several plates in one frame, and a number read off the unit behind this one is
  worse than no number: it will be believed. **If you cannot read a model number
  on this object, `null`.**
- **`whereItIs`** — one clause placing it in the room. *"Against the back wall, left of the two grey vessels."*
- **`partOf`** — **optional.** If this product is plainly a component of another item in this answer, name that item exactly as you named it. *A control head is part of the vessel it sits on. A pressure gauge is part of the manifold it is threaded into.* **Leave it out unless it is obvious** — most things are not parts of anything, and a wrong parent is worse than none.

### `couldNotLocate` — known products you cannot find in any photograph

**This is a real answer and it is wanted.** ⚑ A known product that is not in any photograph usually means **the photograph is missing**, which is a finding about the capture rather than a failure of yours.

Give the product and one clause on where you would have expected it. **Do not stretch to place it.** Saying you cannot see the Franklin cartridge is more useful than pointing at a housing that might contain one.

### `additional` — everything else you can see

For each:

- **`label`** — what a homeowner would call it, in a few words.
- **`classId`** — an id from the supplied class list, copied exactly, **or `null`.** *If nothing fits, `null` and describe it in the label.* Choosing a class that is merely close is worse than choosing none: a wrong class is believed and a missing one is chased.
- **`mediaIds`** — every photograph showing it.
- **`model`** — the model or part number printed on **this object**, exactly as it reads, or `null`.
  ⚑ **Only what is printed on the thing you are naming.** A mechanical room holds
  several plates in one frame, and a number read off the unit behind this one is
  worse than no number: it will be believed. **If you cannot read a model number
  on this object, `null`.**
- **`whatYouCanSee`** — one clause naming what in the picture made you say this. **This is what a person reads beside the photograph.**
- **`whatMakesItDifferent`** — **required, and it is the guard.** One clause saying what distinguishes this from every product on the known list. *"Smaller, white, wall-mounted, no vessel."* **If you cannot say what makes it different from a known product, it is probably that product and does not belong here.**
- **`partOf`** — optional, same rule as above.

### `roomNote`

One or two sentences on what this room appears to be for and how the things in it relate, **where the photographs support it.** Leave it empty rather than narrating something you cannot see.

## Rules that do not bend

**Everything in `additional` is a guess and is recorded as one.** The known products were read and looked up; anything you add is recognised from shape and context. **That is a weaker kind of knowing and the record keeps them apart.** Say less rather than more.

**Never report a thing you cannot see in at least one photograph.**

**Never report the absence of anything** except in `couldNotLocate`, which is about the known list only. *Not seeing a smoke alarm is not evidence there isn't one.*

**Do not assess.** Not condition, not age, not whether something is installed correctly, not what it needs. **You are saying what is there.** What is wrong with it is a separate question asked separately.

**An empty `additional` list is a complete answer.** A room whose known products are all located and which holds nothing else is finished. Do not fill it.
