# The Baseline Process — Standard Detached Home (v2.1)

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Version:** v2.1 — v2 (same day) with four owner corrections: **travel patterns removed from the questionnaire** (§0) · **the floorplan is the priority capture, not an option** (§1.2) · **the shutoff walk-back removed from the Discovery Visit entirely** (§1.4) · **register airflow measurement recorded** (§4.4, new)
**Supersedes:** `HouseSteady_Baseline-Inspection-Process_v1_2026-07-20.md`, which describes a two-visit model with capture and inspection in the same visit. **Renamed** — it is no longer an inspection process, it is the process by which a house enters the service, and the inspection is one part of it.
**Governed by:** `HouseSteady_Baseline-Service-Design_v1-1_2026-07-31.md`. Where they disagree, the Service Design governs.
**What this document is:** the step-by-step field and desk process that populates the Home Binder — from the discovery conversation through handover. Written for a standard detached home: ~1,500–2,200 sq ft, three bed, two bath, full basement, attached garage, forced-air heat. Conditional blocks for well, septic and wood heat are marked ▸ and folded in where they occur, since the region runs heavy on all three.

**What changed, and why.** v1 put capture and inspection in one visit. The first five-zone walk on a real house found they do not fit: capture alone took the visit, and attempting both makes the concierge into an inspector working a checklist in an unfamiliar house. **The three-visit model separates them, and the desk pass between them is what turns a pile of captures into a targeted inspection.**

**Design assumptions:** three visits with a desk pass and a client-facing Home Profile between the first two · structured capture in the field, transcription never done on site · the honesty labels apply to every entry — *not inspected* and *not accessible* are recorded, never papered over · **identification, never assessment**, at every stage.

**Route logic, stated once.** The sequence stays zone-based rather than purely room-by-room, and the reasons survive the redesign: systems cross rooms · some checks need a deliberate order — water run upstairs, ceilings checked below afterwards · exterior work goes first while light and weather hold. **What changed is that the ordering constraints now bind the Inspection Visit**, because that is where the tests are. The Discovery Visit's only ordering constraint is daylight for the exterior.

---

## Stage 0 — Discovery conversation and pre-visit package

**The discovery conversation is a named stage, not a courtesy.** It is where the service is explained, the client's priorities are heard, and — critically — **where the capture is explained before a single photograph is taken.**

**Show the samples.** A sample Home Profile and a sample Binder. *This is what your home's record looks like; the Discovery Visit is how we build yours.* **A concierge photographing a family's belongings is strange without context and ordinary with it**, and this is where the context is supplied. Also on the website.

**Set the attendance expectation.** The homeowner is invited to every visit and expected at none. Sensitive spaces — bedrooms, offices, anywhere they would rather accompany the concierge — are raised here rather than discovered awkwardly on the day.

**Establish who is authorised.** Per the Trusted Circle framework: authority flows from the homeowner's explicit delegation, and payment does not purchase authority.

### Documents to upload or have ready for scanning

Deed or transfer, or survey · any prior inspection report · building, renovation or septic permits · ▸ MECP well record · ▸ septic pumping and inspection records · insurance policy declarations page · equipment manuals, invoices, install records — furnace, AC, water heater, roof, windows · warranties · ESA certificates if any · paint codes if known · anything in the house file drawer — *bring the whole drawer; sorting is our job.*

**This list is the source of the Home Profile's "missing from you."** Documents that have not arrived by the desk pass are asked for on the Profile, which gives the client weeks rather than days to find them.

### Profile questionnaire (10 minutes, online or paper)

Household size · known problems and recurring concerns · comfort complaints — hot or cold rooms, drafts, humidity, odours, noise · renovation history and intentions · DIY appetite, meaning what they would rather do themselves · anything about how the house behaves that a new owner would not know.

**Removed from v1: travel patterns and typical absences.** It arrived for a real reason — checks while the household is away — and **asking a stranger's calendar of absences at intake is exactly what a thief canvassing a house would ask.** It reads as reconnaissance however it is meant, and no explanation repairs a question that has already been asked.

**If away-checks are ever offered, they are requested by the client at the time**, not surveyed in advance. **We check houses, not people**, and the questionnaire is the first place that either holds or quietly stops holding.

**The DIY question earns its place twice now.** It shapes the capture, and it is the opening of the visit-three conversation about which maintenance items they keep.

### Physical prep requests

- Clear access: electrical panel, furnace, water heater, main water shutoff, attic hatch, under every sink, sump lid
- Pets contained for the visit
- ▸ Well households: **no shock chlorination and no plumbing work in the 7 days before the visit** — it invalidates the bacteria sample. Know where the wellhead and any treatment equipment are
- ▸ Septic: locate lids if known; do not park on the bed area
- If shutoff locations are known, be ready to point them out — **unknown is a fine answer and gets recorded as such**

### Scheduling rule (operational, non-negotiable)

▸ **Well households book the Discovery Visit** on days that let the bacteria sample reach the public-health drop-off inside its window — samples are typically accepted only on set weekdays and must arrive within ~24–48 hours of collection. **The local drop-off schedule dictates which weekdays these can run.** Confirm the current schedule before the calendar is built.

**This constraint moved from the single visit to the Discovery Visit**, and the move is an improvement: samples collected on visit one have results back before the Inspection Visit, so the Home Profile can carry them.

## Stage 1 — The Discovery Visit

**One job: capture the house. No tests, no measurements, no checklist.**

### 1.1 · Arrival and the clocks (20–30 min)

The discovery conversation if it has not already happened separately, then **deploy the long-running things first so they run longest**: air monitor and data loggers placed · ▸ radon detector sited · ▸ water samples collected, well and municipal as applicable.

**Deployment and collection are not tests.** They start clocks. Nothing is being operated, assessed or measured.

### 1.2 · The capture circuit

**Exterior first while light holds**, then interior zone by zone.

**The capture routine, which repeats identically in every zone:**

1. **Floorplan the zone. This is the priority capture and it is not optional.** RoomPlan produces the floorplan deliverable, the room dimensions, and window and door measurements in one pass — and the position data that makes the desk pass confirmation rather than data entry. **Every home needs a floorplan and most do not have one**; it is the single most visible thing the Discovery Visit produces.

   **A canvas photograph is the fallback, not the alternative.** Where RoomPlan cannot run, a wide canvas shot keeps the zone usable — and it keeps its own separate job regardless: an orienting shot of a utility room, kitchen or laundry **sent with a trades request**, so a contractor arrives familiar with the workspace.
2. **Move around the room in one direction**, clockwise or counter, consistently.
3. **Capture everything.** Objects, surfaces, concerns, the dirty and the unmaintained, windows, ductwork, anything that will need fixing. **Single-shot capture rather than sweep.**
4. **Note where it matters** — roughly one capture in ten. Voice or text, attached to the capture, and **every note is internal by default.** The desk decides what surfaces.
5. **Standalone commentary** at any point — a voice note with no photograph, for orientation the desk cannot otherwise get.

**Capture access, not only the thing.** Attic hatches, crawlspace entrances, panels behind stored boxes, shutoffs behind appliances. A pole-camera shot through a hatch is worth ninety seconds. **This is what lets the Inspection Visit arrive knowing whether a space is reachable and what tool it needs**, rather than discovering it on a ladder.

**No pinning, no classification, no tagging.** Naming a thing as a component with a type is desk work — it wants a screen, a keyboard, and no homeowner standing in the room.

### 1.3 · The safety exception

**The capture-only rule has one exception and it is not negotiable.** A concierge who encounters something immediately dangerous — a gas smell, exposed conductors, no working smoke alarms, a blocked egress — **acts on it that day.** They tell the homeowner, they record it, and where it needs a licensed specialist they say so and help make the call.

**Deferring a hazard to the Inspection Visit because the process says visit one is capture-only would be indefensible.** The process is a plan, not a permission structure.

### 1.4 · Debrief (15–20 min)

At the kitchen table:

1. **What was deployed and why**, with the sample timeline — bacteria days, chemistry ~1–2 weeks, radon ~3 months.
3. **What happens next:** the Home Profile arrives before the next visit, and it will name what is still needed from them.
4. **The Inspection Visit booked before leaving.**

**No findings are walked at this visit.** Nothing has been tested. A concierge narrating impressions from a capture walk is assessing, and it is exactly the pull the doctrine exists to resist.

### 1.5 · Same-day logistics (30–45 min, off-site)

▸ Samples to the lab or drop-off inside their window — **this drives the day's route.** Capture sync: everything uploaded to the binder builder the same day, while the voice notes still make sense.

**The export is the completion gate.** An inspection is not complete until it is out of the app. AirDrop, Files or USB all satisfy it; it does not need to leave the device over a network.

## Stage 2 — The desk pass

**Four stages. Every one is confirmation of a proposal, never authorship.** That rule is what keeps this to a working session rather than a day's data entry. Full detail in Service Design §5.

1. **Assemble the house** — RoomPlan gives rooms; the concierge says how they connect and which floor. **Produces the floorplan deliverable.**
2. **Place the captures** — with position data this is confirmation; the photographs are already on the plan and the concierge fixes what drifted.
3. **Identify** — AI proposes what things are, reads nameplates, and the concierge accepts, corrects or rejects. Nothing is signed that was not looked at.
4. **Confirm what the engine generated** — maintenance, inspection targets, opportunities, replacement horizons. **This is where the house becomes a plan.**

**Output: the Home Profile, and the session plan for the Inspection Visit.**

---

## Stage 3 — The Home Profile

**Delivered before the Inspection Visit — emailed ahead, or handed over on arrival.**

- **The floorplan**
- **The inventory** — what is in the house, room by room, with photographs
- **The maintenance calendar** — what this house needs and on what rhythm. **One list.** Who does what is the visit-three conversation
- **What we are looking at on the next visit, and why**
- **Missing from you** — documents from the Stage 0 list that have not arrived
- **Any test results already back** — water chemistry where the lab was quick

**It invites correction, and that is a feature.** The client tells you what you got wrong, free, weeks before it reaches a binder.

## Stage 4 — The Inspection Visit

**Targeted. The concierge arrives with a named list rather than a general checklist.**

### 4.1 · What makes an item an inspection item

**This is the test that produces the `scope[]` split.** For every checklist item:

> **Does it require operating, testing, measuring, accessing, or knowing what equipment exists?**

- **No — it is observation and recording only** → **capture item**, Discovery Visit
- **Yes** → **inspection item**, Inspection Visit

**And a second rule that decides the hard cases:** anything whose *content* depends on what was found — a component's own checklist, a test chosen because of what the equipment turned out to be — **is an inspection item by definition**, because the question cannot be asked until the answer to *what is here* exists.

*The item-by-item split is a content pass against the Checklist Master and is owner-authored. This document supplies the test, not the list.*

### 4.2 · The visit

**Collect what was left behind** — air monitor and loggers, now holding weeks of real data. **Radon stays put**, collected and mailed at the ~3-month mark.

**Work the session plan zone by zone.** The plan carries: the targeted inspection list · zone attributes as decided at the Discovery Visit · prior unit photographs for objects being re-shot · carried gaps · monitors due.

**The ordering constraints live here**, because this is where the tests are: the water run threaded through the upper floors with ceilings checked below afterwards · the furnace tested once with its registers verified as rooms are passed · exterior work while light holds.

**Operate the shutoffs** — the homeowner works the water valve themselves once, if safe. This completes the walk-back begun at the Discovery Visit.

**Close every "missing from us" item**, scan straggler documents, re-inspect anything weather-blocked.

### 4.4 · Register airflow — recorded, not specced

**A measure item worth building toward, raised by the owner and recorded here rather than lost.**

Measuring flow at every supply register gives a **balance picture across the whole house** — which rooms are being fed and which are starved. That is:

- **The answer to the questionnaire's comfort complaints.** A client says the back bedroom is always cold; the numbers say it receives a third of what the living room does. **The complaint stops being a matter of opinion.**
- **Cheap to act on.** Damper adjustment is homeowner-level work, not licensed work, so **HouseSteady can do it** — and then re-measure, which is the honest proof it worked.
- **A comparison position by construction.** Same registers, same instrument, next year.

**The line that has to hold, and it is not obvious.** *"Register A reads 45, register F reads 120"* is **Measured**. *"Your system is unbalanced"* is assessment. *"These readings suggest closing the F damper roughly half and re-measuring"* is a **coordinated action with a measured outcome**, which is fine — but **why** a system is unbalanced (undersized return, blocked duct, failing blower) is a specialist question and stays one.

**And the accuracy claim has to be relative, not absolute.** A handheld reading at a register face varies with technique and register geometry, so absolute CFM carries a wide band. **Same instrument, same day, same technique, across every register in the house — the comparison is defensible even where the absolute number is not.** That is the same discipline as `accuracyBand` on a scaled canvas: state the band, never imply precision nobody produced.

**What it needs:** a unit in Table H · a measure item per register, probably riding the existing register checklist item · an instrument decision, where a vane anemometer is cheap and approximate and a flow hood is accurate and expensive · **and the relative-not-absolute rule written into the house style before any of it renders.**

*Not built. Routed to the field track as a change request.*

### 4.3 · The gap report

**This is v1's Phase 11, moved.** It is the Inspection Visit's output, not the Discovery Visit's — because after a capture-only visit nearly every checklist item is unresolved, and **a client document saying "we did not cover 208 things" is not a document.** *(That number is measured, not rhetorical: the first real capture walk produced 213 due and 5 resolved.)*

One page, three columns:

1. **Missing from you** — documents still outstanding from the Stage 0 list
2. **Missing from us** — unresolved items, plus `na` where the config marks the reason as feeding the gap list. What could not be reached or completed
3. **Triggered flags** — specialist assessments and risk-based tests the inspection tripped: electrical triggers, sewer-camera triggers, WETT, septic assessment, asbestos-suspect materials, tap-lead triggers

**Every row is human-reviewed and signed before it renders. Nothing sends itself.** Lab results append as they land, through the edition mechanism.

## Stage 5 — The Handover Visit

**The Binder, and building the ongoing plan together.**

1. **Final render, human QA, handover review** per the Master Spec's Appendix III package. **In-flight items — radon, seasonal tests — shown as *underway* with dates. Never omitted, never claimed done.**
2. **The plan conversation.** Item by item through the maintenance calendar: **which items HouseSteady covers, which the homeowner keeps, what gets scheduled, what gets quoted.**

**This is a working session, not a presentation.** Arriving with the split pre-made presumes the answer and risks the wrong impression — a long list already assigned to HouseSteady reads as an invoice rather than as a plan.

**It is also where the retainer stops being an abstraction**, because it becomes a specific list of things a specific household no longer has to think about.

## Time budgets

**Deliberately absent.** v1 carried per-phase estimates written before anyone had walked a house on this process. **The first real walk showed capture alone consuming a visit**, which is the finding that produced this document — so carrying v1's numbers forward would be carrying forward the assumption they were built on.

**The measurement:** field timestamps for the visits, desk-work timestamps per section for the desk pass (Increment 4 §7). **Ten houses in, the figures exist without anyone having sat and measured them**, and they keep calibrating as concierges get faster.

## Embedded scope decisions

**Policy, not preference. Flagged for sign-off.**

1. **Three visits before a Binder.** Changes the economics session's inputs. Not priced here.
2. **The Discovery Visit produces no client-facing document on the day.** The Home Profile arrives before the Inspection Visit.
3. **No findings are walked at the Discovery Visit.** Nothing has been tested, and narrating impressions from a capture walk is assessment.
4. **The shutoff walk-back does not happen at the Discovery Visit at all.** Candidates are captured, confirmed at the desk, and shown once they are confirmed — Home Profile if the desk is confident, Home Binder if the Inspection Visit had to resolve it. **A confidently wrong shutoff is worse than an unknown one.**
5. **The safety exception overrides the capture-only rule**, always.
6. **The maintenance list is one list.** The split is the Stage 5 conversation.
7. **▸ Well scheduling now binds the Discovery Visit**, which is an improvement — results are back before the Inspection Visit.
8. **Time budgets are measured, not estimated.**
9. **Travel patterns are never asked.** Away-checks, if offered, are requested by the client at the time.
10. **The floorplan is the priority capture.** A canvas photograph is the fallback, not the alternative.
