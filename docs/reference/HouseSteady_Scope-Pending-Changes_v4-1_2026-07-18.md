## Entry 4 — Capacity planning basis: 6 dedicated hours / client / month
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided** *(carried from b)*

Planning basis is **~6 dedicated hours per client per month, plus travel** (was "≈4 hrs all-in" in v4). At 20 clients ≈ 120 client-hours/month ≈ full-time at ~6 productive hours/day. A settled client-month is only ~3 hours of actual work — 6 is a generous internal allowance with headroom for storms, deeper coordination, and buffer.
**Banked implication:** "full capacity = 20 clients" is a comfortable ceiling, not a hard limit — revisit post-density, and see the Plan's *Prove the transfer* phase for how the ceiling lifts.
**Supersedes in v4:** the "≈4 hrs/household/month" figure in *The frame — Cadence*. The "average across the book, never a per-client meter, never sold as hours" principle stands, and now also powers the Ring 3 early trigger (Entry 11).

## Entry 5 — Institutional identity: the named backup, coverage modes, and the promise test
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided**

**Change:** The backup operator is elevated from continuity infrastructure to a **named, client-facing role**. Every household knows two people: **your concierge** (the client-facing role noun) and **a named backup who knows your home**.

- **Mode 1 — visit coverage (routine).** The backup performs the monthly visit: checklist, photos, small work, report input. The judgment layer stays with the concierge remotely — triage decisions, family communication, and anything discretionary go to the concierge by phone, mid-visit if needed.
- **Mode 2 — full coverage (rare, defined).** When the concierge is genuinely out, the backup holds the number: triage and urgent coordination. Decision boundary: **urgent proceeds, novel defers.** Anything within standing preferences and the running list proceeds; anything new or project-tier waits, unless safety says otherwise.
- **Familiarization visit.** One accompanied visit per household within the first **[90] days** — the backup joins a regular monthly visit, meets the homeowner, walks the house. Included; sold as a feature: *two people know your home.* Introduced at Baseline via the Binder's people page.
- **Coverage preference** joins the standing preferences set at Baseline: **substitute** (default — the backup covers, the visit proceeds) or **reschedule** (only your concierge enters; coverage means the visit moves within the month). The homeowner controls who enters their home; the cost of reschedule is flexibility, stated plainly — never dollars. Silence fails toward delivery, per safe defaults. Triage coverage continues either way, so the same-or-next-business-day promise stays honest rather than suspended.
- **Visited-by line** on every report: who performed the visit. Audit trail, normalization of coverage as routine operations, and a running record that the institution served the house.
- **Company number rule.** The public number is HouseSteady's from day one, routed to the concierge — never a personal cell given out raw. The fridge card carries HouseSteady's number.
- **The promise test (language discipline):** any promise only one specific person can keep is a promise we don't make. Kills "a human answers, and it's David" and all kin. Replacement pattern: *"You reach HouseSteady directly — your concierge, or the named backup when they're away; never a call centre."*

**Supersedes in v4:** the Continuity section's infrastructure-only framing (the design carries; the role becomes client-facing).
**Professional-review additions:** *Lawyer* — the agreement names HouseSteady's right to designate a covering operator; consent and access language; coverage preference on the Trusted Circle form. *Broker* — the backup under CGL + bonding; VSC'd before any solo entry.
**Render impact:** who-comes-into-your-home block, Book page, all CTAs, family page, report template, Binder people page, fridge card.

## Entry 6 — The visit envelope: ~2 hours, composition varies, no minutes guarantee
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided**

**Change:** The monthly visit is **a roughly two-hour block: the checklist first, the sit-down review (the meeting), then whatever small work fits that day** — composition varying by month, chosen together at the agenda. Some visits the house demands two hours of looking; most leave time to tinker.

- **Small work is source-agnostic:** noticed during the visit or drawn from the running list — no billing distinction between them. The perverse incentive (mention the hinge in advance and it costs money) is dead.
- **The agenda is the valve.** At triage, *we* name which list items ride this visit's block; the client doesn't unilaterally load it.
- **No duration warranty.** Client-facing language is descriptive ("we're there about two hours"), never a promised quantum — a guaranteed number just recreates the metering problem. The promise is the trajectory: **chunk by chunk, the list shrinks and the home gets better.**
- **List-hour redefined:** dedicated time beyond what the visit absorbs — *"the visit absorbs what it can; the list-hour is when you want more, on purpose."* **Doesn't bank; no grace period** (resolves the v4 open).
- **Render note:** keep the word **"meeting"** for the sit-down — the kitchen-table review is the teamwork frame physically operating.

**Supersedes in v4:** the "60–90 minutes" cadence figure in *The frame*; the "~30 min small-task cap"; the "noticed things are while-I'm-here / listed things are the list-hour" boundary in the rate card; the list-hour 90-day-grace open item.
**Render impact:** What-we-do, How-a-month-runs, Pricing, sample report (its "small jobs from your list — included" language is now correct rather than contradictory).

## Entry 7 — Away months convert: the check schedule
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided**

**Change:** When a household is away, the monthly visit **converts** into the agreed check schedule — up to **[weekly]** check-ins on a defined vacancy checklist (heat, water, windows and doors, signs of entry), logged and reported like everything else. **Same fee, same reports, same one number.**

- **Framed as conversion, never accounting.** The client line: *"Away for a month or a season? Your visit becomes your check schedule — same fee, same reports, same one number."* Never "the fee covers the checks."
- **Snowbirds become a designed path,** not an edge case: every away month runs in check mode; the service travels with the season.
- **Cadence confirmation (advocacy principle, fourth instance).** The client or their broker confirms the policy's required cadence **in writing**; we document and deliver the agreed schedule in the Home Record; our reports are their proof of checks done. We never interpret the policy and never warrant that the insurer is satisfied. Kills the site line "a schedule that keeps your insurer happy."
- **Departure prep is the companion step.** Ontario policies commonly require checks every 24–72 hours during heating-season absences *unless the water is shut off and plumbing drained* — so departure prep (water off, winterize; done at a visit or coordinated) is what typically brings the required cadence inside the included schedule. Offered at triage before every extended absence; broker confirms the resulting number either way.
- **Beyond [weekly]: quoted.** The floor exists so the promise has edges; with departure prep in play it will be invoked rarely, not never.
- **Partial months need no machinery:** the visit lands when they're home, checks cover the away stretch, triaged like everything else.

**Supersedes in v4 / Entry 3:** the unbounded "seasonal/vacancy check-ins are INCLUDED" gains these edges and this mechanism.
**Render impact:** What-we-do (away/snowbird section), Pricing, FAQ.

## Entry 9 — Intake gating & the waitlist
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided**

**Change:** The public region stays **"Bay of Quinte"** — brand descriptor untouched. **Intake is area-gated:** the intake flow asks the town; the internal open zone routes to intake, everywhere else routes to the waitlist. No public boundary is drawn; nobody is told they are founding anything.

- **Copy spine is route-based and mechanism-descriptive** — the mechanism is the subject, never coverage. Locked gate copy:
  > *"HouseSteady runs on routes — clustered, regular visits, so every home gets proper time. Across the Bay of Quinte, intake opens area by area, route by route. Join the list for [your town]: the list is how an area opens, and you'll hear from us the moment yours does."*
- **Honesty edges:** never volume claims ("due to high demand"), never time promises ("soon"), never staff nouns (no "concierges" plural, no role nouns at all in gate copy).
- **The waitlist is the demand instrument.** Signups map demand by area — the expansion-sequencing intelligence. **Dispatch threshold:** an area opens when **[N]** households cluster within a routable area, or when adjacent-route capacity reaches them. Internal number.
- **Internal open zone at launch: Belleville and north** — an operating variable that moves with density, never a public fact.

**Render impact:** intake/booking flow logic, waitlist page, homepage and footer service-area language (any "serving Belleville, Trenton, Napanee, PEC…" list becomes the route framing or the bare descriptor).

## Entry 12 — Pet exception closed
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Status: Decided**

**Change:** **Hard no** on feeding or care of animals by our hands — the emergency top-up included. Resolves the v4 open call.

- Rationale for the record: a priced exception becomes backup pet care in a family's mind within one season.
