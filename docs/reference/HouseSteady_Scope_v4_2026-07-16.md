## The frame — four commitments the whole service hangs on

**Cadence.** One monthly anchor visit, 60–90 minutes, in a route-assigned recurring slot — the client picks a preference at onboarding; we own the calendar. Internal all-in budget ≈ 4 hrs/household/month (visit + drive + report + phone/coordination) — an average across the book, never a per-client meter, never sold or described as hours.

**Checklist doctrine.** Every check is a defined, bounded, logged checklist. We report what the checklist covered on the date visited — never an open-ended promise to notice everything. A vague "we keep an eye on things" creates an expectation and arguably a legal duty; a logged checklist protects both sides and makes the value visible.

**The announce-first keystone (agreement commitment).** Free→paid is always announced and quoted before the work — never a surprise bill. This single commitment is what lets the boundary lines below stay slightly soft at the edges without risk: when a job grows past its category, we flag it and quote it before continuing.

**The advocacy principle.** HouseSteady acts on the client's behalf and never owns a third party's performance. We advocate, coordinate, chase, and document; we do not warrant the trade's work, the utility's response time, or the landlord's timeline. Stated once here, applied throughout — trades, urgent coordination, landlord/condo liaison.

## The Core boundary — three rings (written into the agreement)

**Ring 1 — never metered.** Phone triage, routine trade coordination stacked onto route days, and urgent incident coordination. The call is the product; the client must never hesitate to dial.

The urgent promise, plainly: **call us first, every time — the coordination always happens; we attend when we safely can.** On every urgent call the coordination starts immediately: triage by phone or text, shutoff guidance from the Home Record, and contact with whichever utilities, trades, or emergency services the situation needs. On-site attendance is conditional — when it's safe, when we're available, and when it's within the service area. Most urgent situations are handled fully by phone and coordination; there is no promise to drop everything and appear.

Hours, stated plainly: call or text anytime; triage and response same or next business day. Explicitly not 911 and not a 24/7 line. **Life-safety goes to 911 and the utility first, HouseSteady second** — the fridge card carries this in human words ("Something urgent at the house? Here's the order of calls.").

*Vocabulary note: "urgent incident coordination" is agreement and internal language only; client-facing material keeps the human wording above.*

**Ring 2 — flat and disclosed, quoted before work.** Off-route attendance at a specific requested time, the handyman list-hour, tech installs, seasonal checks, supplied consumables (our cost, never marked up — see the rate card), and project coordination. The urgent split: the urgent phase — triage, shutoff guidance, dispatch, make-safe coordination — always belongs to Ring 1; once the house is safe, the recovery (remediation trades, adjusters, rebuild coordination) is a project, quoted on a calm day.

**Ring 3 — book-level valves.** A chronically heavy household is a signal, not a billing event: upsell the second visit or the list-hour, project-ize the backlog, or reprice at the annual household review — the respectful valve built for exactly this. Never a surprise mid-month bill.

## How the month runs — the shared list

Every household runs on one shared artifact: **the running list** — every observation, to-do, and idea, triaged together.

- **Rolling intake.** Clients and family send items as they come up through the month — message or email preferred for routine things, the phone always welcome. For anything urgent, the phone stays first-class, always: Ring 1's "never hesitate to dial" is untouched by this preference.
- **Every item gets an owner, in conversation:** HouseSteady / homeowner / family / an outside solution / back-burner. Triage is something we do together, not a queue we disappear into.
- **The pre-meeting agenda.** Before each visit, the current list goes out as the meeting agenda — inspection scope, the list to review, and any odd jobs doable that day. The full loop, every month: **agenda → visit → report.**
- **Nothing falls off silently.** Items assigned outside HouseSteady keep their assignment date and are re-raised at every meeting — silence fails toward re-discussion, never toward forgotten. Safety-relevant items carry our written recommendation not to defer; the decision stays the homeowner's, and the advice is documented. (The checklist doctrine's cousin: report what was seen and said; never own what they chose.)
- **Family can raise; family cannot authorize.** Intake is open to the whole Trusted Circle; the approval rungs still govern (see Trusted Circle).
- **Batching is a choice, never a delay.** Accumulating list and tech jobs is always offered as the client's saving — "as they come, or bundled next month: one visit, one disruption" — never an imposed wait. Time-sensitive items get caught at triage.

**The monthly focus (year 2 — populated from discovery).** Beyond the standing rhythm, each month can carry a single theme, in two lanes. An *our-hands focus* — one deeper task worked into the visit, within the cap: door and window hardware one month (hinges, latches, weatherstripping), detector batteries another, caulk-and-seal another. And a *coordinated focus* — a book-rate batch of one outsourced service across the book (gutters, window cleaning, tree pruning, duct cleaning), run on the book-rate mechanics already defined, opt in or out at triage. Year one *discovers* which tasks actually recur — the running lists and the private time log surface them — and year two turns that into a set monthly-focus calendar. The focus also gives each monthly report a headline, feeding the value story. *(Deliberately not pre-built from assumptions: the book teaches the calendar.)*

## The monthly visit — what we check and handle

*This section is the source the Baseline Visit Checklist and the Monthly Visit field card render from — they quote it, never restate it.*

**The standing checklist** (logged, every visit):
- test smoke & CO detectors
- furnace filter and humidifier pad check/change — labour included; consumables client-supplied, or provided at our cost if opted in (see the rate card)
- water heater visual — leaks, rust, corrosion at fittings
- under-sink checks, kitchen and baths
- sump pump check (in season), floor drain eyes
- visual on roofline, shingles, eaves, downspouts — from the ground
- grading/foundation eyes — pooling, new cracks, downspout discharge
- windows & doors — locks, weatherstripping, screens
- test GFCI outlets
- quick attic/basement eyes — moisture, pests, smells
- house-specific weak-point list (built at Baseline, checked every visit)
- warm-season items: exterior taps on, hose bibs, AC season-change flags
- cold-season vigilance (logged, every winter visit):
  - furnace, dryer, and HRV exhaust vents clear of snow — a genuine carbon-monoxide risk
  - ice dams and dangerous icicles before they tear off a soffit
  - **visible heavy accumulation on roof / deck / carport after a heavy dump — photographed and noted; structural questions referred out.** Snow reachable from the ground (and not covered by the client's snow-removal contract) is raked down as far as we safely can — ground only, never on the roof, never up a ladder. A dedicated post-storm roof-snow pass runs as a storm pass (see rate card).
  - pipes in unheated spaces during cold snaps
  - sump discharge line not frozen ahead of the February thaw
  - backup heat / generator confirmed working before ice-storm season, not during
  - exterior taps off, hose bibs drained (fall); eavestrough flag after leaf-fall (cleaning coordinated)
- visit-day walkway safety: clear and salt the path in use (salt client-supplied, or at our cost if opted in), noted in the report — documented courtesy, inside insured operations, never an off-book favour
- photo log during every visit

**Small tasks inside the visit cap (~30 min) — noticed things:**
- bulbs, batteries (incl. detector batteries), clocks — labour included; materials client-supplied, or at our cost if opted in (see the rate card)
- bins to curb / recycling sorted on visit day
- hang a picture, tighten handles/hinges, adjust a sticking door
- reset breakers and GFCIs
- program thermostat, fix the TV input, remote/phone/tablet basics
- move light items — one-person lift only
- ground-level holiday touches: shrub lights, railing garland, wreath — nothing on a ladder

## The Home Record & reporting

Everything we check, supply, coordinate, and recommend lands in one place: the **Home Record** — a named, homeowner-owned product, not a filing habit.

**What it is.** Two halves: the physical binder that lives at the house, and our digital master. Together they hold the measured property profile, equipment models and serials, filter sizes, shutoff locations, paint colours, the full service and contractor history, and every photo report.

**Built at Baseline:**
- measured property profile — driveway and walkway dimensions, window count, key measurements: measure once, never re-quote, and every measurement deepens the record
- shutoffs labelled — water main, gas, breaker map

**It's theirs, and it's portable.** A complete copy of their household's record is available on request, anytime — no lock-in, no data held hostage. *(The reconciliation with the "data asset" language in the pitch: the record is portable on purpose. The moat is that people won't* want *to leave, never that they can't. The strategic asset is the* aggregate *— route knowledge, service histories, and trades intelligence across the whole book — which powers the later-phase play, not any one client's file. A consent line covering aggregate, de-identified use of service data goes in the agreement; see the appendix.)*

**The reporting rhythm:**
- **After every visit** — a photo report to the homeowner, shared with exactly who they choose.
- **Every month** — a one-page summary carrying a running **year-to-date value strip**: items supplied and dollars saved vs. retail, trips avoided, visits done, tasks handled, and plan progress. This is the answer to "what am I paying for in a quiet month," delivered every month rather than argued once a year.
- The render starts as a one-page report from simple admin software and graduates to a dashboard when the book justifies it — contents designed now, tooling built when manual becomes the bottleneck.

**The Annual Home Plan** lives as a living section of the Home Record. Drafted at Baseline — honestly labelled a first pass that deepens over year one as visits teach the house — it holds now / soon / later priorities, seasonal tasks, and equipment ages with likely replacement windows. Language is always planning estimates, never warranties of timing: "the furnace is 18 years old; typical life is 15–25," never a promised date.

**The annual review** is not a separate document — it's the anniversary month's report plus a review meeting, run on the client's **service anniversary** rather than the calendar, so the value summary and the pricing conversation land together by design. Order matters: **first** the value walk-through (money and time saved, the year in review), **then** next year's plan and pricing — the respectful Ring 3 repricing valve. This is where a heavy household gets repriced, in daylight, never by surprise.

The loop, closed: monthly report (value strip + plan progress) → annual review (value walk-through, then plan refresh + pricing). Every visit has a larger purpose on paper.

## Coordinated work — we arrange, oversee, report

We line up, oversee, and document the work; a third party performs it and bills the client directly — never through us, never marked up. Per the advocacy principle (see the frame), we advocate and chase but never warrant the trade's work or own its deficiencies.

**Routine vs. project — the line, and the keystone that lets it stay soft at the edges:**
- **Routine** = one standard service call with a known or preferred contractor, price confirmed, stacked onto a route day, completion confirmed and documented. Ring 1, never metered.
- **Project** = the structured quote process (up to three quote requests in a similar format; three or fewer presented depending on commitment; reviewed with the client), *or* any single job that outgrows a normal service call. Ring 2 — a flat fee quoted before work starts, scoped to our role through completion: light-touch (line up, hand off) vs. heavy (regular attendance, progress checks, advocacy on issues). The same job can carry a different fee by depth of role — always quoted first, per the keystone.
- **The client always does the approval and signs** — ties directly to the bright line: we never sign on the client's behalf.
- **Deficiency-chasing is routine until it tips** — a dispute, dragging across weeks, or a second trade needed to fix the first's mess. Announced when it tips (the announce-first keystone). Situational judgment is the trigger, never a counter.

**What we coordinate:**
- all licensed-trade work: plumbing, electrical, HVAC, gas, roofing, structural
- routine service calls: gather quotes, schedule, meet the trade on site, oversee, photograph results, chase warranties/callbacks/deficiencies
- meet deliveries, technicians, inspectors
- housecleaning, laundry service
- lawn care, snow removal (seasonal contracts) — where a snow contract covers the roof, roof snow is coordinated to that contractor rather than raked by us
- eavestrough cleaning
- junk removal, movers, downsizing help
- rides and transportation (incl. subsidized community options where the household qualifies)
- grocery and meal delivery setup
- pest control
- pet care via a vetted sitter — we arrange and oversee (homeowner's own video useful for vendor oversight); narrow exception: a one-time documented emergency top-up while the sitter is being arranged *(this exception is still an open call — confirm it or make it a hard no; tracked on the roadmap)*
- holiday lighting — push permanent smart eave lighting as a one-time coordinated install (project revenue, zero recurring ladder risk, colour/scheduling changes handled remotely or while-I'm-here); our hands do ground level only
- emergency recovery phase (post-make-safe): remediation trades, insurance documentation, rebuild coordination — runs as project coordination
- bill / rebate / grant navigation and application help — every figure verified before it's promised

**Cross-book batch coordination — the book rate.** When many households need the same seasonal service (e.g. twenty houses needing gutters cleaned), we line up one vendor across the book at a batch rate — a big job the vendor wants, a better price every client gets. The vendor bills each client directly at the batch rate, **never marked up** — the savings pass through entirely (the "book rate," a membership benefit). Inclusion runs on the standing-preference pattern: offered at triage, easy in or out. Per client it's still routine coordination (Ring 1) — one standard call with a preferred vendor; one negotiation serves twenty households. *(Strategically this is Door Two opening on its own — the vendor who lands a twenty-house batch answers our calls first — but nothing about the client offer depends on that. A seasonal batch calendar, spring and fall, is the natural extension as the book grows.)*

## The rate card — flat, disclosed, plain dollars

No credits, no tokens, no banked hours. Numbers live here and here only; where a number is still open, the roadmap tracks it.

- **Baseline (onboarding, one-time)** — a 2–3 hr walkthrough: binder started, property measured, fridge card built, shutoffs labelled, key agreement signed, bill & rebate review — **$[setup]**. Two doors: pay the setup and stay month-to-month, *or* fold it into a 12-month term (then it drops to month-to-month).
- **Core retainer** — flat monthly, **~$300** (founding-client rate, honoured for the early book; stated standard rate **$[329–375 — pick one]**).
- **Supplied consumables** — across every routine consumable (furnace filters, softener/conditioner salt, detector batteries, bulbs, humidifier pads), the labour to install or swap is **included**; the material is client-supplied, or provided **at our cost, never marked up**, if opted in. Opt in as a standing preference at Baseline for predictable items; ad hoc otherwise. No floor — a $3 bulb is recorded and billed at cost, because the record is the proof, not overhead. Itemized on the monthly invoice, receipts available; feeds the year-to-date value strip (items supplied, $ saved vs. retail, trips avoided). Ring 2.
- **Handyman list-hour** — attached to the visit day, up to 1 hr worked from the client's running list (caulking, flappers, weatherstripping, mounting, seasonal swaps) — **$[X]/mo** standing, or **$[Y]** ad hoc. Doesn't bank *(90-day grace: open)*. Two-person items book a double slot or wait for a second set of hands. Boundary: noticed things are while-I'm-here (included); listed things are the list-hour.
- **Off-route attendance** — meeting a trade at a specific requested time, off route days — **$[Z]** flat. (Stacking onto a route day is always free.)
- **Second mid-month presence visit** (shorter) — **$[X]/mo**.
- **Seasonal checks** — vacancy / snowbird, at insurance-required cadence — **$[X]** per visit; standing preferences set at onboarding.
- **Storm pass** after major weather — **$[X]**; standing preference set at onboarding. Includes a ground-reachable roof-snow rake where wanted and not covered by a snow contract.
- **Tech installs** — leak sensors, video doorbell, smart thermostat, smart smoke/CO, Wi-Fi troubleshooting, permanent eave lighting (coordinated) — quoted flat.
- **Tech Baseline / Tech Sweep (one-time project)** — a single comprehensive pass across the household's technology: computers, internet and Wi-Fi, TVs and streaming, phones, tablets, and any smart-device or upgrade opportunities — assessed, upgraded or replaced where wanted, set up, and recorded in the Home Record. The logic mirrors the Home Baseline: do the heavy tech work once, and ongoing tech help afterward is lighter for everyone. Quoted flat, scoped before work. *Bounded on purpose — a defined project, not an open-ended IT-support commitment; ongoing tech help after the sweep stays within the small-task cap, the list-hour, or a fresh quote. The money-and-credentials bright line applies in full: we set up devices and connectivity, never operate financial accounts or take custody of banking credentials (password handling during device setup is a grey-zone item flagged to the lawyer).*
- **Tech hour (standing add-on)** — for the household whose appetite for tech outruns their comfort with it: up to an hour of unhurried, patient tech help attached to the visit day, worked from the running list — the phone that "broke," the TV that switched inputs, email, photos, video calls, the printer — taught at the client's pace, as many times as it takes. **$[X]/mo** standing, or **$[Y]** ad hoc; doesn't bank. The boundary mirrors the list-hour: quick tech basics during the visit stay inside the small-task cap (included); listed or recurring tech help is the tech hour; device rollouts and whole-home changes are the Tech Sweep or a quoted install. Pairs naturally with the Sweep — sweep once so everything works and is documented in the Home Record, then the tech hour keeps it working and builds the client's confidence. The money-and-credentials bright line applies in full.
- **Project coordination** — roofs, furnace/AC, accessibility mods, any multi-trade or multi-quote job, emergency recovery — flat fee quoted before work starts, **never a percentage of the job**.

## Bright lines — what we will not do, in any form

- personal care of any kind — bathing, dressing, toileting, feeding, transfers, mobility assistance
- anything medication-related — reminders, sorting, administering, disposal, custody
- health or wellness monitoring, medical advice — we check houses, not people
- drive clients anywhere
- errands or shopping runs
- handle cash, bank cards, PINs, or online banking; never pay bills from client accounts (navigation is not money-handling)
- sign contracts on the client's behalf; no power-of-attorney-type duties
- hold or transport valuables, firearms, or medications
- licensed-trade work with our own hands — including anything gas, including pilot relights
- **the roof and ladder rule:** no ladder work above step-stool height; never on the roof surface; no hand-cleaning eavestroughs. Ground-based tools that reach the roofline — a roof rake for snow — are fine; the rule is about never accessing the roof and never going up a ladder.
- solo heavy lifts — two-person rule by policy over [X lbs / awkward-item list], regardless of who's strong; the job is designed so no hire ever needs to be a strongman
- 24/7 emergency response, security monitoring, or any guarantee about the gaps between visits — checks, not monitoring
- pet feeding/walking as a standing service (see coordinated sitter above)
- tenant or rental-property management *(distinct from the apartment/condo tier's liaison role — see that section)*
- hands-on pest remediation
- hazardous-waste handling or transport
- renovation labour of our own hands
- body-worn cameras — the protection stack is the photo report, the lockbox log, the doorbell record, the VSC, and bonding/insurance; a dashcam in the vehicle: yes

## Operating rules

- **Safe defaults.** Standing service preferences — storm passes, vacancy cadence, snow choices, batch-service opt-ins — are set once at onboarding. Silence always fails toward "deliver per the standing preference," never toward skip. A notification is an exception-handler, not a dependency.
- **Make-goods stay in the ecosystem.** A missed or sub-par item is fixed with a free extra visit or a waived add-on — logged and metered, so generosity never quietly papers over a quality pattern. Cash refunds are the last resort, not the reflex.
- **Language discipline.** Coordinate / oversee / report — the word "monitor" never appears, for the home or anything else. Winter and roofline items report *visible heavy accumulation*, never "assess" and never "load" — we report observations, not structural judgments.
- **Keys and access.** A rotating-code smart lockbox on site; per-visit and per-trade codes, logged. Paired with the homeowner's own video doorbell, this is a complete access audit trail — access events, not living rooms.
- **Cameras** are always the homeowner's choice and the homeowner's footage. No tier ever requires one.
- **Accompanying.** At the home during contractor visits — yes. To appointments or stores — no.
- **Heavy households** are handled at renewal or by upsell (Ring 3), never by surprise billing.
- **Rate card** lives on one page; every price is flat and disclosed before work.
- **Two-person lift rule** by policy over [threshold — open], regardless of who's strong; the job is designed so no hire ever needs to be a strongman.
- **At first hire:** non-solicitation + NDA (client list, pricing) — never a non-compete (presumptively unenforceable in Ontario). An Ontario employment lawyer drafts it.

## Trusted Circle — captured at Baseline

The homeowner names their circle at onboarding, on a form: who receives reports, who are incident contacts, who may approve work, who is informed-but-cannot-authorize, preferred contractors, and a do-not-share list.

**The authority ladder — four rungs:**
1. **Informed** — receives reports / is kept in the loop.
2. **Informed-but-cannot-authorize** — sees everything, approves nothing.
3. **Reviewer** — named by the homeowner to be *consulted* before certain decisions (project-tier jobs, or anything over a dollar figure the owner picks — defined on the form). Consultation, not transfer: the owner's decision stays final unless approval is explicitly delegated. (Joint sign-off is possible if the owner shapes it that way — but always named as delegation, never assumed.)
4. **May approve** — holds approval authority, *because the homeowner explicitly delegated it on the form.*

**Headline principle over the whole ladder: payment does not purchase authority.** Approval never flows from who pays; every rung exists only because the owner granted it.

**Defaults so it never jams:** urgent coordination never waits on a reviewer. For routine items, the form states whether work waits for the advocate or proceeds after a stated window. The circle is revocable anytime, and the annual review re-confirms it (this is also where consent-revocation is handled).

## Continuity — designed now, executed before meaningful client count

The one number can never go dark, and no client's record can live in one person's head.

- a named **backup-operator** role, with defined coverage scope and access
- **phone routing** so the single number is always answered or triaged
- a **vacation-coverage message** that either keeps the same-or-next-business-day promise honest or explicitly suspends it, with notice
- **dual record access**, without casual credential sharing

The *design* is settled here; the *execution* — finding and vetting the backup, standing up the routing — lives on the roadmap's pre-launch checklist, in place before the book grows. (Selling note: "what happens if you're sick or away?" is the first question an adult child asks. The written answer is trust infrastructure, not just ops hygiene.)

## Apartment / condo tier — parked, written, not the founding target

*Marked parked: this tier is designed and sequenced for later activation, not launch. It exists here so the model is complete, not because it's next.*

- **Venue-based guardrail (the test is the building, never the resident).** Independent apartment and condo buildings only. Retirement residences with care services are out *as venues* — even for a fully independent resident inside one. The building type decides, never the person's condition.
- **One shared core, a single fork in coordination.** The in-suite checklist is a smaller-footprint variant of the monthly visit — detectors, leak points, fan-coil/HRV filters, caulk & seals, balcony condition — roughly 30–45 min. The fork:
  - **Renter →** landlord/building liaison: log the issue, submit it with photos, chase it, document it — along the landlord-vs-resident duty line (Ontario RTA). Coordinate private trades only for what the building won't cover.
  - **Condo owner →** house-style in-suite coordination for the unit's own guts, plus condo-corp liaison for common elements. Alterations that need building consent (balcony bird deterrents usually do) get it first.
- **The advocacy principle, third instance.** We act on the client's behalf and never own the landlord's or condo corp's performance — the same rule applied to trades and to urgent attendance.
- **Leak sensors matter more here** — a unit leak is a neighbour claim.
- **The ladder rule still applies on balconies.**
- **Rate** follows the smaller time budget (~2–2.5 hrs/household/mo): **$[150–200]/mo**, set at activation.
- **Density is the prize:** multiple clients in one building means near-zero travel between visits — "building days" are the best route economics in the book, and lobby word-of-mouth moves fast.
