**Region:** the Bay of Quinte. Intake opens area by area, route by route; the internal open zone at launch is Belleville and north — an operating variable, never a public fact.

## The institutional doctrine

**The client's relationship is with HouseSteady. Every household has a named concierge and a named backup; the house's knowledge lives in the Home Record, not in anyone's head — and any promise only one specific person can keep is a promise we don't make.**

How it operates:

- **Role noun:** client-facing, the person is **"your concierge"** — matching the brand descriptor (the company is *Home Concierge*; the person is *your concierge*), warm, and scaling without a rewrite the day "your concierge, [name]" is someone else. The backup is **"a named backup who knows your home."**
- **Voice:** company voice ("we") in every render. David appears in exactly two capacities — as **founder** (the story, told once, inside the who-comes-into-your-home block) and as **your concierge at launch** (stated as fact where relevant). Never as the promise.
- **No claimed team.** Never plural staff, never implied headcount through imagery. The institutional claim is made through **mechanisms** — the named backup, the familiarization visit, the coverage preference, the visited-by line, the Home Record — never through implication.
- **The number is HouseSteady's** from day one, routed — never a personal cell given out raw. If twenty households save "David's cell," the institution can never take the number back.
- **The two-layer honesty.** "Productized, not personal" is true of one layer and unproven of the other, and this plan says so. The **field layer** — checklist, route, report, small work — is productized and teachable today. The **judgment layer** — triage, family communication, advocacy, discretion — is being *proven* transferable, deliberately, from client one (see *Prove the transfer*). The plan no longer papers over the difference.

The backup role's full mechanics — coverage modes, the familiarization visit, the coverage preference, the visited-by line — are specified in the Scope's pending entries. The point here is strategic: **the backup is the first visible proof of institution**, and the written answer to the first question an adult child asks.

## Mission

- Help people stay safely and independently in the homes they love.
- Be the single trusted contact for everything the house needs — chosen by the homeowner, never arranged around them.
- Give the homeowner, and the people who love them, confidence that the home is checked, documented, and acted on.
- Coordinate the work; never do personal or medical care.

## Vision

- Own the **coordination layer** for an aging region's homes and the trades that service them.
- Concierge = demand-sensing + trust engine. Trades network = supply + asset play. **One thesis, two doors.**
- Scale path: solo operator → proven transfer → clustered territories → regional layers.
- Long game: multi-concierge operation + vetted trades network + growth-consulting / equity / roll-up with aging-out trades.

## Target customers — the overlap

**One household. One person who feels the problem; one person whose consent is required.** The adult child often feels the urgency; the homeowner holds the authority — and our own first principle (*payment does not purchase authority*) means we can never sell past the homeowner. So the addressable market is not the demographic tailwind. It is the **overlap** where need and dignity coexist:

- the homeowner sees a real benefit;
- accepting the service doesn't feel like surrendering independence;
- the family respects the homeowner's control;
- the retainer is comfortably affordable;
- the need is meaningful but short of personal care.

**Positioning doctrine** (elevated from banked seeds — these are operating rules, not campaign ideas):

- **The guilt bridge.** Homeowners think *"I don't want to bother the kids — they're busy"* while the kids say they want to help. HouseSteady dissolves the standoff as the documented intermediator: nobody is bothered, nothing is hidden, everyone sees the same report.
- **The teamwork frame.** Never replacing the proud homeowner: organizing together, tackling together, outsourcing together. The shared running list *is* this frame, operating — and the kitchen-table meeting inside every visit is it, physically.
- **Ordering rule:** homeowner dignity first, family reassurance second, in every render, every time.

**Wider funnel, by design:** snowbirds are now a designed path, not an edge case — the away-month conversion means the service travels with the season. Busy downsizers and anyone who wants the house handled can buy without any age story.

**Market tailwind (investor data, never a label):** ~44,000 residents 65+ across Hastings & Prince Edward counties (24.1% / 35.2% of population vs 19% nationally); Belleville–Quinte West metro grew +7.5% 2016–21 vs Ontario +5.8%. Alternatives are fragmented — no local category leader. *(StatCan 2021; the 44k combines the two county figures — re-derive at verification, before anything goes external.)*

## The service — characterized here, specified in Scope

HouseSteady sells one thing: a household's property needs handled through a single trusted contact, on **one flat public monthly rate ($[rate — economics session]), nothing upfront, month-to-month.** The retainer buys four anchors:

- **Presence** — a monthly visit of roughly two hours: the defined, logged checklist first, the sit-down review, then whatever small work fits that day — noticed or from the list, chosen together at the agenda. The promise is the trajectory, never a quantum: *chunk by chunk, the list shrinks and the home gets better.* When the household is away, the visit **converts** into the agreed check schedule — same fee, same reports, same one number.
- **Access** — one HouseSteady number for anything house-related, triaged same or next business day; expressly not 911 and not 24/7. Urgent coordination is always included; on-site attendance is conditional — when it's safe, when we're available, when it's in the service area. Two named people can answer for the house: your concierge, and the named backup.
- **Coordination** — routine coordination included: we line up, oversee, and document the work; trades bill the client directly, **never marked up** — including cross-book "book rates" that pass through entirely. Larger jobs, multiple quotes, and ongoing oversight are project coordination: scoped and quoted before work begins, never a percentage.
- **Proof** — one monthly report (visited-by line, findings with photos, the running list, the year-to-date value strip, plan progress); the **Home Binder**, homeowner-owned and portable, deepening with every visit; and the annual meeting, where the year's value is walked first and next year's plan and pricing second.

**Trust economics are the strategy, not a compliance layer:** no markup, ever; flat disclosed fees, quoted before work (the announce-first keystone); checks, not monitoring; the homeowner holds the authority — reports go to exactly who they choose, cameras are always their choice, and **payment does not purchase authority**; hard bright lines fence off personal and medical care, money handling, licensed-trade work, and roof/ladder access.

Every mechanism — the three rings, the visit envelope, the conversion model, the coverage modes, the rate card, the bright lines, the Trusted Circle ladder — is specified in the **HouseSteady Service Scope** plus its pending entries. Nothing in this plan overrides it.

## The product ladder

1. **The standalone Home Baseline** — one-time, real-priced ($[X]), no subscription required, **fully creditable toward the retainer within [90 days]**. The low-commitment door: the homeowner who can say yes to *"let's get the house organized once"* but not to an indefinite relationship — and the adult child who can propose the first without insulting anyone. Also the referable object for launch partners, and a Binder in a non-subscriber's kitchen as a standing advertisement.
2. **The retainer** — the spine. Everything above, one flat rate, month-to-month.
3. **Add-ons** — the handyman list-hour, the tech hour, the second monthly visit, tech installs, the Tech Sweep, at-cost supplied consumables. Real additional labour, flat and disclosed.
4. **Project coordination** — roofs, furnace/AC, accessibility, recovery-phase work: flat fee, quoted first, never a percentage.

Ladder logic: each rung earns the next, and **the Binder travels the whole ladder** — started at the Baseline, deepened by the retainer, referenced by every project.

## Revenue model

*Structure decided; numbers open. Every figure below is bracketed pending the economics session — see that session's mandate at the end of this section.*

- **Phase 1 target: 20 clients × $[rate]/mo = $[recurring]/yr** recurring, plus standalone Baselines (additive when they don't convert; credit-absorbed when they do) and add-on margin.
- **Capacity basis:** ~6 dedicated hours per client per month, plus travel (per the Scope's pending entries). At 20 clients ≈ full-time. A settled client-month is ~3 hours of actual work; 6 is a deliberate allowance. 20 is a comfortable ceiling, not a hard limit — and *Prove the transfer* is how the ceiling lifts without the founder leaving the field.
- **The founder time log runs three buckets, not five:** **field** (visit, travel, checklist, small work) · **judgment** (triage, family communication, advocacy, coordination decisions) · **network** (vendor scoring, batch-building, succession intelligence). Hours now map to the three future roles, so the transfer decision and the first hire are designed by the data, not guessed.
- **No trial period — planning assumptions, instrumented.** Value lands on day one; growth is never gated behind a pilot and no client is ever framed as one. Churn is tracked by month of tenure.
- **The retention answer is designed in, not hoped for.** The watch-point is months 6–12, when the backlog clears. The answer ships with the service: the monthly report's value strip answers "what am I paying for" every month; the Home Plan gives every visit a forward purpose; the annual meeting walks value first, then plan and pricing. And Ring 3 now has an **early trigger** — two consecutive heavy months opens a scope conversation then, not at month eleven; mid-year is scope-only, repricing waits for the anniversary.
- **Recurring is the spine.** Baselines and add-ons are margin on top — and heavy add-on households double as trades deal-flow signal.
- **The exit math is deliberately unstated here.** Plan v3's take-home estimate was built on revenue lines that no longer exist (setup fees, storm/vacancy billing, a founding/standard split). It is not re-stated until it is re-based. **The economics session's mandate:** pick the single rate and the Baseline price; reset the add-on card (the outside read flagged the provisional numbers low); build the full operating-expense schedule (insurance & bonding, VSC, lawyer, accountant, vehicle & mileage, phone & software, marketing, backup coverage cost, working-capital contingency); then re-derive take-home at 20 clients and test it against the **$52,500 freedom number**. The exit decision runs on that math, not this paragraph.
- Self-employed structure: home office, mileage, write-offs. *Confirm with an accountant.* HST treatment must be explicit everywhere a price appears.

## Launch plan

- **The gauge, re-instrumented.** Primary: **[5] signed households within [60] days of marketing launch.** Meetings are tracked as a funnel metric only — a meeting validates curiosity; a signing validates the business. Supporting instruments: 90-day retention · measured hours per settled household (three-bucket log) · Baseline→retainer conversion rate. What the gauge triggers is the founder's call alone.
- **The funnel is transparency.** Marketing states what you get, what it does for you, and what it costs — the price is public from day one: **the website qualifies; the meeting closes.** Client-facing, the meeting is framed as what it honestly also is: *a practical fit conversation about your house and your first priorities* — never "nothing left to sell you," which protests too much.
- **Channels — D2C is the first lane:** the website, Facebook, CJBQ radio, and local expos — where David's reach, reputation, and skillset already live. Radio and Facebook drive people to the website; Facebook additionally *shows* the service — what a visit caught, what the Binder holds, what a quiet month still delivered. Expo discipline: the venue does the age-targeting; booth copy names the situation, never the age.
- **Referral partners, split into two tiers:**
  - **Tier 1 — active at launch: the non-care channel.** Estate lawyers, insurance brokers, financial planners, downsizing realtors. Their referral arrives in *our* frame — "get the house handled," "get the house documented" — and **the standalone Home Baseline is what they refer:** a clean, finite, referable object. Armed with the partner one-pager (build-and-bank already on the roadmap; now launch-active).
  - **Tier 2 — established-phase: the care channel.** Care coordinators, OTs, discharge planners. Unchanged rationale from the prior plan: a care-channel referral arrives pre-framed as elder-support no matter what the one-pager says. D2C and Tier 1 keep frame control.
- **Intake gating & the waitlist.** Intake is area-gated; the waitlist is the demand instrument, mapping signups by area — the expansion-sequencing intelligence. **Dispatch threshold:** an area opens at [N] clustered households, or when adjacent-route capacity reaches them. The gate copy is locked in the Scope's pending entries; its honesty edges (no volume claims, no "soon," no staff nouns) are language-rule matters, not style preferences.
- **Word-of-mouth is built into the service, not bolted on:** the shared list, the book rate, and the monthly report are the stories neighbours repeat — no "referral program" required.
- **The onboarding wave:** hitting the gauge means Baselines at 2–3 hours each stacked on the most eventful client-months in the book — staggered deliberately across a calendar the founder controls.
- **Pre-launch runway.** Day-one items that can't iterate: the service agreement, insurance & bonding, the VSC, the bright lines, **the named backup (recruited, vetted, VSC'd, insured — the spec lives in the Scope's pending entries)**, and the HouseSteady-routed number. Everything workflow-and-polish iterates through the first months. The human-situation protocols gate the first client, not the first ad — and they now gain a client-facing render, because *"what do you do if I don't answer the door?"* is a selling answer, same class as the backup answer.

## Growth path

- **Phase 1 — Prove:** solo, ~20 clients, a documented and teachable operation. The exit decision is gated on settled evidence, not signup count: ≥[15] settled households · [6] months of measured retention · proven hours per settled household · a costed operating-expense schedule · functioning backup coverage · conversion at the public rate.
- **Phase 2 — Prove the transfer** *(renamed from "Clone," which presumed the answer)*. The question this phase answers: **can trust move from the founder to another HouseSteady person without the book noticing?** The machinery is already running from client one — the named backup, familiarization visits, coverage events, the visited-by line. **The gate:** coverage events occur across the book and the book holds — no coverage-triggered churn pattern, retention through substitution ≥[95%], comfort confirmed at annual meetings. Only past this gate does hiring-to-grow proceed. **If transfer proves, the 20-client ceiling lifts without the founder leaving the field:** a second field-layer operator extends capacity under the same judgment layer — and the three-bucket time log is what says when. First hire terms: non-solicitation + NDA, never a non-compete (presumptively unenforceable in Ontario; an Ontario employment lawyer drafts it).
- **Phase 3 — Cluster & layer:** non-overlapping territories; density cuts travel and raises margin; area-lead roles emerge.
- **Phase 4 — Coordinate:** multi-concierge + a vetted trades network; visits → report review → overseeing reviewers.
- **Expansion pack (post-density, separate strategy doc):** managed property-maintenance lines — robotic mowing, density-gated tractor snow, credit-bank pricing — drop into clusters the concierge already built. **Never lead with it.** Credits belong there, never to the concierge.
- **The economic fact underneath every phase:** unit economics *improve* with density — the 100th client in a cluster is cheaper to serve than the 10th.

## Strategic edge

- **Acute pain, two-sided demand** — the person who feels the problem and the person whose consent is required are both reachable, and the service is built to serve the overlap rather than sell past the homeowner.
- **Trust is the operating system and the acquisition strategy — the moat is the accumulated proof.** The policies (no markup, flat fees, homeowner authority) are copyable in a week; what compounds and can't be copied is the record of honouring them: years of clean reports, Binders in kitchens, references, continuity through coverage events, route density, vendor relationships, retention. We build the policies for the client and the *proof* for the moat.
- **Productized where it's true, honest where it isn't.** The field layer is a checklist, a route, and a report — teachable today. The judgment layer is being proven transferable on purpose, with a gate. That honesty is itself an edge: the scale plan runs on evidence, not on a slogan.
- **The aggregate is the asset — and it's only an asset if it's structured.** Each household's Home Record is portable by design; the moat is that clients won't *want* to leave. What compounds is the aggregate: route knowledge, service histories, and a live map of the local trades landscape. **The trade scorecard makes the claim true:** logged from the first coordinated job — response time, quote-vs-final, punctuality, quality, callbacks and deficiencies, warranty response, capacity signals, succession notes — structured (Airtable-shaped), never founder impressions. *(Aggregate, de-identified use is covered by the consent line in the agreement — Scope appendix.)*
- **The related-party rule, seeded now** — Door Two's conflict, pre-answered in writing before it exists: if HouseSteady ever holds an interest in a trade, **ownership is disclosed to the client; the client's freedom to use anyone is untouched; the owned trade is held to the same scorecard and standards as every other; the coordination fee never varies by vendor chosen; and major projects are offered an independent alternative.** The roll-up must never spend the trust that created it.
